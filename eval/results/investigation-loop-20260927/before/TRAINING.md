## Intended capability versus observed results (September 22)

The machinery capability envelope records developer-authored contracts separately
from compiler outcomes. Expected construction is not a positive training label;
an expectation conflict is not a proved bug. Unknown prerequisites, absent caller
wiring and a failed candidate do not establish impossibility. Perfect component
implementation does not imply complete model search or all-input semantic proof.

The first read-only export reassesses 100 existing observations across 30 worlds,
with zero new compiler trials. All capability and investigation artifacts remain
training-ineligible; the empirical transition model and model weights are not
updated. Existing test-only before/exact fixtures are excluded from assessment
inputs. See `docs/CAPABILITY_ENVELOPE.md` and
`eval/results/capability-envelope-20260922/RESULT.md` for contracts, provenance,

## Automatic scheduling-policy development (September 22)

`eval.search_evolution` proposes bounded numeric search policies from observed
compiler progress, then selects by complete replay without losing incumbent
exacts. This updates a search policy artifact, not model weights or repair code.
The paired laboratory gate requires an additional certified match at equal
budgets, preserves previously accepted evaluation matches, and rejects incomplete
or infrastructure-failed observations. Candidate artifacts freeze before their
separate evaluation panel is run.

The pilot under `eval/results/search-evolution-20260922/` uses previously exposed
SBK1 development functions. These records remain training-ineligible. Function
disjointness within the experiment does not make them fresh holdouts; the
vibrato siblings also cross partitions. Its deterministic proposal algorithm is
developer-authored and does not establish improvement of a learned researcher.
Production promotion and model training remain separate decisions.
The first completed run selected greedy search on 3/4 research exacts versus the
parent's 2/4, then rejected it on a separate development comparison: 1/5 exacts
versus 2/5 for parent and beam. No generation advanced. This is an exercised
rejection/retention mechanism, not a positive RSI or model-learning result.

The follow-up treats this rejection as development evidence. Wobble's original
evaluation world is explicitly wrapped as a development regression; it is not
silently relabelled research or placed in model training. The proposer now samples
the geometric midpoint of its observed penalty bounds, and selection can filter
policies that lose registered development successes. On fresh compiler runs the
selected revision preserves all five previously known exacts and improves the
nine diagnostic cases from 4/9 in 240 calls to 5/9 in 152 calls. These cases informed
the revision and do not measure transfer. Four separate exposed development
cases produce no exacts for any control or candidate. The 806 new receipts remain
training-ineligible; model weights, production defaults and S0 are unchanged.
Details and receipts are in `eval/results/search-evolution-20260922/ANALYSIS.md`.

## The sequencing constraint

You cannot train anything useful until the loop runs, because **the loop is what produces
the dataset**. Public decomp data gives you (asm → matched C) pairs, and the published
result for training on exactly that is ~1.2% byte-exact with fine-tuning barely beating
zero-shot. That road is already mapped and it is a dead end.

What does not exist in any public corpus — and what your loop generates for free — is
**refinement data**: given a candidate C, its compile result, and its instruction diff,
what edit raises the score. Nobody's pretraining set contains that. That is where a
trained model has real headroom.

So: build the loop, log everything, then train on your own trajectories.

---

## Tier 1 — No training (do this first)

**Best-of-N against the verifier.** Sample N candidates, compile all of them, keep the
highest scorer. Because the verifier is perfect and cheap, this is training-equivalent
capability for zero training cost — it is rejection sampling with a ground-truth reward.

Also in this tier, all free:
- Retrieval from the KB into the prompt (known types, sibling functions in the same TU,
  previously matched functions with similar shapes)
- Few-shot examples drawn from already-matched functions in the *same* TU
- Feeding the instruction diff back verbatim rather than summarizing it

**Measure this before considering anything below.** If best-of-20 plus KB retrieval
already clears the Phase 2 gate, training is an optimization, not a requirement.

## Tier 2 — SFT on the refinement step

> **Status 2026-09-20 (final) — Tier 2 was run on a synthetic curriculum, independently audited,
> and its headline was RE-MEASURED through a corrected oracle and CONFIRMED.**
> `eval/results/local-posttraining-20260920/RESULT.md` carries the full account at the head of the
> file, with receipts in the same directory (`evaluation-certified/`).
>
> A LoRA adapter was trained on 29 compiler-verified repair tasks (loss 0.133 → 0.042, 392/392 LoRA
> tensors changed) and evaluated on 27 family-disjoint frozen held-out tasks at equal budget:
> **19 object-exact against the baseline's 4, gained 15, lost 0.** An audit then showed the exactness
> check compared only the `.text` section, **which excludes relocations** — two functions differing
> only in their callee (`external_a` vs `external_b`) produce byte-identical `.text` and the check
> called them equal, while `solver.byte_certificate.certify()` correctly calls them different.
> Reproduced, and the verdict now comes from the certificate everywhere.
>
> **The number survived the correction, and that is measured rather than argued.** The dataset was
> rebuilt under the certificate (still 56/56 verified repairs, zero leakage, same 29/27 split) and
> the evaluation re-run: baseline 4/27, adapter 19/27, 54 calls per arm. Across all 108 draws the
> number where `.text` matched but the certificate **rejected** is **0** — structurally, because the
> generator emits exactly one external symbol name and only two families call anything, so a model
> cannot summon a second callee name that compiles. The oracle was genuinely unsound in general; it
> did not inflate this particular figure. The adapter was also re-pinned by recomputation
> (`evaluation-certified/ADAPTER-IDENTITY.md`), because retraining is not bit-identical and an
> earlier draft quoted a digest that never matched the artifact.
>
> The other two audit findings are closed too: the manifest is now schema 3 and binds the dataset
> bytes, every record and the rendered prompt (`eval/frozen_manifest.py`), and the promotion gate
> requires a complete equal-budget `test` panel instead of accepting 12 matching ids with zero
> baseline draws.
>
> What does **not** transfer, and must not be quoted as capability: this is a **synthetic**
> curriculum — object-level exactness in a standalone translation unit, not a ROM-backed matched
> function — and the gain is concentrated on the width/immediate axis (narrow-locals 14/16,
> subtract-to-narrow 2/2, split-initialiser 3/8, while-form 0/1), largely absent on register
> allocation. Transfer to real game functions is **untested**. One useful oracle-independent
> observation stands: the baseline's failures are dominated by **C89 syntax errors** (`static
> inline`, which IDO rejects) rather than by semantic distance.
>
> **The game-function route still has no dataset.** The local 7B checkpoint produced **0** improving
> children in 56 verified repair attempts while compiling 92% of them, and the project's own sealed
> holdout sets consume 89% of the observed improvement supply (237 of 265 improving edges). A
> lineage audit (`eval/results/local-posttraining-20260920/AUDIT.md`) found the reason is coverage,
> not mislabelling: 94.2% of parented rows in the knowledge base store no prompt at all, leaving
> **139** usable verified pairs from 8,132 edges. That is why this stage trained on the synthetic
> corpus, where verified answers exist by construction, rather than on game trajectories.

Not on the draft step. The draft step (asm → C) is what has already been shown not to
benefit much. The refinement step is different:

```
input:   current C  +  compile result  +  instruction diff  +  score
output:  revised C  (that scored higher)
```

Mine these from explicit `attempt_edges`: a parent/child pair where the child improved
is a training example. Row order is not lineage — best-anchored refinement can branch
from an older attempt, while best-of-N samples are independent roots.

**A score decrease is NOT a negative example** (corrected 2026-09-20). This file used to say the
opposite — "attempts that *decreased* the score are negative examples". That was an assumption, not
a measurement: an intermediate edit can lower instruction similarity and still enable the eventual
exact match, so turning every drop into a rejected example teaches the model to avoid the moves that
reach a solution. `eval/repair_states.classify` therefore marks a drop `unresolved` unless something
independently demonstrates the branch is dead (a compiler refusal, or an explicit dead-end marker).
Keep the drops; do not relabel them.

## A separate task format: choosing the next ACTION from an observation (2026-09-21)

Source-repair SFT teaches the model to write C. This is a different task with a different input, a
different output and its own dataset, and it is deliberately a separate format rather than a change to
the repair path:

```
input      tool definitions + current observation + prior action/result history + budgets
completion the next assistant action JSON, including its arguments
```

| piece | module | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| labels | `eval/tool_action_dataset.label_for` | rules over OBSERVABLE fields only (verification state, no-effect-since-change set, declared `needs`, budget), so a grader can re-derive every label without a model or a compiler |
| fixtures | `eval/tool_action_dataset.procedural_exercises` | paired states where one field changes and the right action changes with it; malformed results, cancellation, blocked infrastructure, missing prerequisites, exact certificates, exhausted budgets |
| execution-backed | `eval/tool_action_collect.py` | snapshots a TRAIN-split state, executes alternative applicable actions from INDEPENDENT COPIES under the same ceilings, records what each actually did |
| splitting | `eval/tool_action_dataset.freeze_splits` | whole FUNCTION FAMILIES per bucket; families already reported on are pinned to dev |
| combination | `eval/tool_action_manifest.py` | one target per acceptable alternative (capped, `stop` never added by expansion), then bounded per-family weights; writes the manifest |
| trainer | `eval/train_tool_action_sft.py` | completion-only CE; the trained span is `ids("{") + ids(completion[1:]) + EOS`, built with the same separate-prefill call inference uses |
| evaluation | `eval/tool_action_eval.py` | three arms, mechanical grading, two ablation levels, costs |

**The compilation contract is part of the task.** The controller compiles the initial candidate and
every transformed source and attaches the whole verdict to the step; the observation states
`ALREADY COMPILED` or `UNVERIFIED`. A `compile` against an already-verified source is a redundant slot,
and that is what the "redundant compiles" metric counts — training and grading use the same rule.

**The masking is the experiment, so it is hand-written.** Prompt, system, history and every tool result
are `-100`; only the action JSON plus the prefilled `{` and EOS carry loss. Verify the boundary with
`token_report.json`: in this dataset **all 138** examples have a one-call tokenization of the completion
that differs from the two-call span, which is why the trainer must not tokenize the completion in one
call. Also note what low loss is worth here: 138 examples reached 0.067 in three epochs, and that is
memorisation, not evidence of a policy (see `eval/results/tool-action-20260921/REPORT.md`).

## Multiple verified children per repair state (2026-09-20)

Compilation is many-to-one, so a state's target is an **equivalence class of sources** and the
recorded child is one member. Measured on the certified run: of 32 exact draws, **10 are
substantively different programs producing the identical object** — one dropped
`extern s32 syn_ext(s32);` for `typedef`/`s32_t` spellings, another moved a trailing `return 0;` into
a `default:` inside the switch. Training on the recorded child alone penalises a model for finding a
different member of a class the oracle accepts.

| piece | module | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| state identity | `eval/repair_states.state_id` | hashes **target + parent + feedback + build + prompt context**. Never the function id: two records for one function with different parents are different states, and `assert_states_are_not_functions` refuses a grouping that collapsed to ids |
| harvest | `eval/target_augment.py` | proposes source rewrites of each **train** answer and keeps only what `certify_exact` accepts. A rewrite is never *assumed* neutral; 82 accepted over 29 tasks. Refuses any split but `train` — harvesting the held-out panel would change what is being measured |
| weighting | `eval/repair_states.balanced_weights` | one unit per **function**, split across its **states**, split across its **novelty classes**. Cosmetic duplicates collapse to one class, so they cannot inflate weight |
| objective | `eval/train_source_repair.py --balanced-weights` | `L(x) = -sum_c w_c * log p(c \| x)`, completion-only |

**What this does NOT claim.** Cross-entropy over several verified children reweights evidence that is
already in the dataset. It cannot make the model explore, and it cannot reach a solution the data
does not contain. Diversity of *sampled output* is a diagnostic, not the objective; certified matches
within a fixed budget are the objective.

**Normalisation, stated exactly.** HuggingFace's loss with `labels` and `-100` masking is a mean over
completion tokens **pooled across the batch**, so (a) a longer completion already carries more
gradient than a short one before any weighting, and (b) a per-example weight is only exact at
`batch_size == 1`. The trainer **refuses** a weighted run at batch size > 1 rather than approximating
silently. Weights are scaled so the dataset's mean weight is 1.0; without that they would divide
every gradient by the example count and the run would look like a much smaller learning rate — a
failure that reads as "weighting did not help".

**Split isolation.** Variants inherit the split of the task they were verified against, and
`load_task_examples` only ever joins a variant to its own record. Evaluation discoveries never enter
training.

- LoRA on a 7–14B code model is sufficient; this is a narrow, highly patterned task.
- Expect it to learn compiler-specific idiom repair: IDO register allocation habits,
  branch-shape choices (`for` vs `while` vs `do/while` vs `goto`), signed/unsigned
  slips, `s32` vs `int` typedef choices, temp-variable count and declaration order.
- Those are exactly the things that decide the last 5% of a match and are close to
  invisible semantically.

**Guard:** hold-out discipline is per *function*, not per attempt. All attempts for a
given function go entirely into train or entirely into eval, never split across.

## Tier 3 — RL against the compiler

This is the correct end state, and it is what the prior-art author concluded after SFT
underdelivered. The setup is unusually favourable:

- **The reward is dense.** objdiff gives 0–100, not pass/fail. Mainstream decompilation
  RL work has to fall back on coarse "re-executability" because it lacks ground truth.
  You have per-instruction agreement.
- **The reward is free and exact.** No reward model, no LLM judge, no human labels.
- **Reward shaping is natural.** Score delta per edit, with a terminal bonus at 100.

GRPO fits well: sample a group of candidate edits from one state, score all of them,
advantage against the group mean.

**Hard prerequisite: a fast Oracle.** RL calls the reward function constantly, so the
Phase 0 acceptance target (cached score under 50ms) is not cosmetic — it is what makes
Tier 3 tractable at all. If scoring is slow, this tier is closed to you.

## The economic argument for a small model

Independent of quality. A run over ~5,000 functions at ~50 iterations each is ~250,000
model calls. At frontier prices that is the dominant project cost.

Target architecture once Tier 2 works:

| Role | Model | When |
|---|---|---|
| Bulk refinement | distilled 7B, fine-tuned on your trajectories | default path, most iterations |
| Escalation | frontier model | on stall, on CHALLENGE, on novel TU |
| Drafting | m2c (not a model at all) | always first |

Route by score and iteration count. Most refinement steps are mechanical and a small
tuned model handles them; the interesting ones are where you spend real money.

## What to log NOW so this is possible later

The `attempts` table must capture enough to reconstruct a training example without
re-running anything. If Phase 2 logs less than this, Tier 2 and 3 are unreachable
without redoing the work.

Per attempt, required:

- `func_addr`, `run_id`, `iteration`, and `parent_attempt_id`
- Full `source_code` of the candidate (not a diff — the whole thing)
- The **exact prompt context** that produced it, or a content hash plus enough to
  rebuild it deterministically (KB snapshot version, retrieved examples, m2c output)
- `compiled` (bool) and, on failure, the compiler stderr
- `score`, and the full instruction-level `diff_summary` — not just the number
- `strategy` — which prompt/tactic generated this attempt
- Model identifier and sampling parameters
- Wall time and token cost
- Source/prompt hashes, raw pre-extraction response, extraction status, and stop reason
- The edge action and exact feedback used to derive each child attempt

The explicit parent → child edge is what turns this into refinement data, so **never**
infer a pair from adjacent rows, overwrite an attempt row, or prune failures. Failed
and regressive attempts are the negative examples.

**Enforced, not documented, since 2026-09-20.** `solver/llm.GenerationReceipt` is a data
structure with one terminal status per model call, and `eval.trajectory_factory` records it on
every attempt. What this bought, measured on a 172-call pilot: 172/172 rows carry the full
prompt, the raw response, the model, the digest, the sampling parameters, token cost, wall time,
extraction status and stop reason — against 29 historical `factory-refine` rows that carried
none of them. Three defects were found only by auditing real rows rather than mocks:

- a generator that returned `list[str]` and dropped the meta dict (fixed: `draw` returns
  proposals that carry receipts);
- a generator that attached no parent to repair draws, so 8 repairs were stored with
  `parent_attempt_id` NULL and no edge (fixed: the FACTORY attaches the state, because
  parentage is a property of the request);
- a fixed `seed` passed for every call, which the service treats as fixed-seed sampling, so
  68 "independent" draws were 17 distinct generations repeated four times (fixed: seeds are
  derived per `(function, role, draw index)`).

Audit any collected batch with
`python -m eval.collect_repairs --verify --scratch-db <db> --out x --plan`, which asserts per
repair that the parent exists, that its source and its own diff appear verbatim in the child's
prompt, and that the edge row exists.

## Contamination rules

The first target is a game already decompiled to ~100%, so its source may be in
pretraining data. This invalidates evaluation silently if unmanaged.

1. Prefer **obscure** targets over famous ones. SM64 and Ocarina of Time source is
   certainly in pretraining corpora; a model may reproduce them from memory and your
   held-out numbers become fiction.
2. Never place held-out reference source in a prompt, including as a few-shot example.
3. Sanity check for memorization: prompt the base model for a held-out function with
   *no* asm context. If it produces something close to the real source, that function
   is contaminated — drop it from the eval set and note how many you dropped.
4. Report the contamination-check result alongside every match rate.

## Decision points

- Do not start Tier 2 until Phase 2's gate is cleared with Tier 1 only.
- Do not start Tier 3 until the Oracle meets its latency target and Tier 2 has shown
  measurable gain.
- If Tier 1 alone gets you where you need to be, ship that and skip the rest.
